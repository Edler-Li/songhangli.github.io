# Project Overview

This summer research project develops a repeatable workflow for fabricating and characterizing **polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) piezoelectric sensors**. The work spans electrode-pattern design, screen printing with silver paste, sample mounting, low-noise signal conditioning, and controlled mechanical testing.

The long-term goal is to compare electrode-fabrication methods and quantify the electrical response of PVDF under known loading conditions. A central research question is whether **laser-induced forward transfer (LIFT)** can provide a lower-thermal-budget alternative to electron-beam evaporation for patterning electrodes on temperature-sensitive polymer films.

# Research Objectives

- Fabricate repeatable PVDF samples with controlled electrode geometry.
- Build a fixture that converts programmed test-machine motion into repeatable deformation of the sample.
- Measure the transient voltage or charge generated under mechanical loading.
- Reduce environmental interference, particularly 60 Hz pickup and cable-related noise.
- Establish a path toward comparing LIFT and evaporated electrodes using matched patterns and active areas.
- Extend the characterization to material-phase analysis and piezoelectric coefficients such as $d_{31}$ and $d_{33}$.

# Measurement Strategy

PVDF can be characterized through either its dynamic piezoelectric response or its static capacitance change. The choice of readout determines what physical behavior can be observed and which error sources dominate.

| Feature | Dynamic voltage/charge readout | Static capacitance readout |
|---|---|---|
| Monitored variable | Voltage or charge generated by changing stress | Capacitance changed by compression |
| Input signal | $V(t)$ or $Q(t)$ | Sensor capacitance, $C_s$ |
| Typical response | AC-coupled or high-pass behavior | DC-coupled or low-pass behavior |
| Best suited for | Impacts, vibration, acceleration, and transient loading | Persistent load, position, and proximity |
| Dominant error source | Charge leakage and finite input resistance | Parasitic cable capacitance and electromagnetic interference |

The first test configuration focused on the **dynamic response**, using a charge-amplifier or piezo-amplifier front end and an oscilloscope to capture the waveform generated during repeated loading cycles.

# Sample and Electrode Design

The sample stack combines a PVDF film, patterned silver-paste electrodes, copper tape, conductive leads, and a rigid 0.8 mm PVC support. The electrode layout was iterated to support three sensing electrodes, a shared ground, and flexible-wire connections while keeping the active geometry reproducible between samples.

![KLayout electrode-mask designs for PVDF samples]({{ site.baseurl }}/images/projects/pvdf/electrode-mask-layout.png)

The electrode patterns were parameterized in **KLayout** using reusable cells and macros. The design includes 5 cm sample boundaries, lead routing, and multiple pad configurations for rapid fabrication trials. The same layout can be reused for matched LIFT and silver-paste samples so that electrode pattern and area remain controlled during comparison.

## Fabrication Workflow

1. Design the electrode mask and lead geometry in KLayout.
2. Cut the screen-print mask; an initial laser setting of speed 2000, power 85, and one pass was tested.
3. Print silver-paste electrodes onto the PVDF film.
4. Attach the flexible leads and ground connection.
5. Bond and align the sample stack, then inspect the finished interfaces under a microscope.
6. Mount the sample in the mechanical fixture and connect it to the signal-conditioning board.

Two three-electrode silver-paste prototypes were produced during the initial fabrication cycle. Testing these samples exposed practical layout issues—including lead spacing and ground-pin orientation—that informed the next mask revision.

# Mechanical Fixture and Test Bench

The fixture was drafted in **Onshape** and prepared for 3D printing. Its purpose is to constrain the PVDF/PVC strip while converting controlled compression into measurable bending. Treating the support and PVDF layers as approximately linear over the test range allows the deformation to be related to the programmed compression depth.

![Early fixture and deformation concept]({{ site.baseurl }}/images/projects/pvdf/fixture-concept.jpg)

An **ADMET eP GaugeSafe** test controller was configured to apply repeatable loading cycles. One initial program used:

- Segment A position: **7 mm** at speed **300**
- Segment B position: **1 mm** at speed **300**
- Pause between cycles: **4 s**

For each loading speed, the planned analysis is to capture an equal-duration waveform, extract the peak voltage, and compare peak response against time and actuator speed. This creates a direct link between the mechanical input and the measured electrical output.

# Signal Conditioning and Noise Control

Early dry tests showed strong **60 Hz interference**, making shielding and grounding part of the sensor design rather than an afterthought. The measurement chain was developed around a charge-amplifier PCB, a piezoelectric laboratory amplifier, coaxial cabling, and battery power to reduce coupling from mains-powered equipment.

The front-end resistor and capacitor values determine both gain and settling time. Further tuning is required to shorten recovery between loading events without filtering out the PVDF signal of interest.

# Initial Result

![Oscilloscope capture of repeated PVDF loading cycles]({{ site.baseurl }}/images/projects/pvdf/oscilloscope-response.png)

The oscilloscope capture shows a repeatable response synchronized with successive mechanical loading cycles. The waveform includes sharp transition features followed by a slower recovery, consistent with a dynamic piezoelectric signal passing through a finite-time-constant measurement chain. This result verifies the complete path from mechanical actuation through the PVDF sample and analog front end to oscilloscope acquisition.

# Next Steps

- Redesign the lead spacing and place the ground connection on the same side as the output pads.
- Fabricate matched samples for a controlled comparison of electrode-deposition methods.
- Sweep loading speed and compression depth while extracting peak voltage from equal time windows.
- Tune the amplifier resistance and capacitance to reduce settling time.
- Improve shielding, grounding, and cable management to suppress 60 Hz noise.
- Use FTIR and XRD to examine PVDF phase composition.
- Measure or estimate the $d_{31}$ and $d_{33}$ piezoelectric coefficients.

# Research Outcome

The project established an end-to-end experimental platform for PVDF characterization: parameterized electrode masks, screen-printed prototypes, a custom mechanical mount, controlled cyclic loading, and a working electrical readout. More importantly, the first measurements identified the design variables that will determine the quality of the final comparison—electrode geometry, fixture repeatability, amplifier time constant, and environmental noise.

These foundations enable the next phase of the research: quantitative comparison of fabrication methods under matched mechanical and electrical conditions.
